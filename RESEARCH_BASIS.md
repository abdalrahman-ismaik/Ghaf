# Ghaf Research and Content Basis

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-26
**Purpose:** Evidence and culturally grounded content for Feature 003
**Product status:** Research basis, not medical, legal, psychological, or religious advice

This document translates evidence into product rules. It does not authorize diagnosis, promise
behavior change, issue a religious ruling, or replace review by UAE parents, educators, child-safety
specialists, Arabic speakers, or qualified legal and religious advisers.

In this document, capitalized **Parent** means the authorized parent-or-guardian product role, not
only a biological parent.

## Research Conclusions

1. Use rewards to scaffold a routine, not to purchase obedience or permanent affection.
2. Make tasks specific, positive, achievable, and observable.
3. Give prompt human recognition; keep symbolic Seeds predictable and secondary.
4. Support autonomy, competence, and relatedness through choices, attainable steps, and family
   meaning.
5. Never remove earned progress, shame a miss, or make a virtual tree die.
6. Compare each Child with their own goal; use a combined household garden and coarse, opt-in
   family-circle milestones based only on eligible Green Impact activity.
7. Apply `visibilityScope` and `circleEligible` before any shared visual or counter update. Keep prayer, kinship, affection,
   food consumption, wellbeing, hygiene, disability-related routines, reflections, media, and
   Parent notes out of cross-family views.
8. Use bounded AI for task support. Do not create a companion, therapist, religious authority,
   emotion detector, or diagnostic system.
9. Treat the UAE living landscape as a product metaphor and separate symbolic growth from measured
   environmental impact.
10. Use synthetic profiles and prepared media for SMAC; real child data requires a separately
    reviewed production design.

## Evidence to Product Rules

| Evidence area | What the evidence supports | Ghaf rule | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positive reinforcement | Clear achievable behavior, prompt rewards, praise, and gradual phase-out can support behavior; earned rewards should not be confiscated. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/using-rewards.html), [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/Positive-Reinforcement-Through-Rewards.aspx) | Confirm promptly, award predictable Seeds, and never deduct them. | CDC page focuses on younger children; age adaptation is required. |
| Descriptive praise | Naming what happened helps a Child understand success; warm general praise also matters. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/using-rewards.html), [Leijten et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2016.09.008) | “You sorted the bottles and asked before carrying the bag—that kept the job safe.” | Do not generate robotic praise after every tap. |
| Token systems | A review of 24 K–5 studies found meaningful short-term behavior effects. [Kim et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/01454455211058077) | Use Seeds to establish a routine, then fade frequency. | Evidence is mainly school-based and does not prove lasting character change. |
| Autonomy support | A 238-sample meta-analysis linked autonomy-supportive parenting with child well-being across cultures and developmental periods. [Bradshaw et al.](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0001389) | Offer Parent-approved choices, explain why, allow renegotiation, and let help count. | Choice remains bounded by safety and family context. |
| If–then planning | A 2026 meta-analysis of child studies reported a small-to-medium average effect on goal attainment, with high variation. [Breitwieser & Reinelt](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41784001/) | Let the Child set cues such as “After dinner, I carry my plate to the sink.” | Do not overclaim that a plan guarantees habit formation. |
| Intrinsic/extrinsic balance | Expected tangible rewards can crowd out intrinsic interest, especially for activities already enjoyed. [Deci et al.](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/1999_DeciKoestnerRyan_Meta.pdf), [Cerasoli et al.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035661) | Reward new or resisted routines most; fade points for stable or inherently meaningful activity. | Kindness, creativity, prayer, and affection should not become permanent transactions. |
| Prosocial behavior | Material rewards reduced later spontaneous helping in one young-child experiment; social praise did not. [Warneken & Tomasello](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/AmericanPsychologicalAss/Warneken_Extrinsic_DevPsych_2008_1554561.pdf) | For kinship and kindness, emphasize gratitude, reflection, stories, and human acknowledgement rather than a persistent transaction. | One experiment does not define every family or age group. |
| Growth-oriented feedback | Strategy/effort praise supported persistence better than fixed-ability praise in a classic child study; later growth-mindset effects are modest and context-dependent. [Mueller & Dweck](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/Mueller_Dweck.pdf), [Yeager et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1466-y.pdf) | Praise planning, persistence, improvement, and appropriate help-seeking. | Never say “good child,” “smart child,” or praise empty effort disconnected from a strategy. |
| Cooperative gamification | Gamification has small positive average effects; motivational results vary, and combined cooperation/competition performs better than pure competition in some contexts. [Sailer & Homner](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w) | Default to cooperative family goals and private self-progress. | Do not claim a leaderboard is evidence-based for every Child. |
| Positive discipline | The AAP finds yelling, humiliation, and harsh punishment ineffective or harmful. [AAP policy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30397164/) | No public failure, point fines, sarcasm, red shame states, dying trees, or “lazy/defiant” labels. | Ghaf is not a behavior-treatment program. |
| Food autonomy | Child-feeding guidance discourages pressure to finish food or using food as reward/punishment. [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/nutrition/Pages/The-Clean-Plate-Club.aspx), [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/food-nutrition/how-to-raise-healthy-eater) | Reward portion planning, food care, table help, and waste reduction—not amount eaten, calories, or body size. | Parents remain responsible for food safety and dietary needs. |

## Age and Ability Defaults

| Product band | Task presentation | Reward rhythm | AI surface | Social surface |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6–8 | One task at a time, 1–3 visual steps, optional prepared audio playback, two choices | Fixed award only for eligible acquisition tasks | Curated controls; no free text or real voice | Own progress plus eligible combined household goal |
| 9–11 | 2–4 steps, if–then cue, choice of order, optional evidence/reflection | Fixed award only for eligible acquisition tasks | Structured Coach intents/template input and prepared media | Private self-comparison; combined household canopy |
| 12–14 | Co-authored goals, rationale, substitutions, longer projects; optional evidence/reflection | More maintenance/recognition-only tasks and longer milestones | Guardian-enabled bounded text/voice with stronger privacy control; never unrestricted chat | Guardian-managed, Child-assented eligible Green-action aggregate only; age never overrides prohibited categories |

These are interface defaults, not developmental judgments. A Parent must be able to shorten a task,
turn off audio, remove evidence, allow extra time, specify assistive support, and choose “completed
with help” without reducing dignity or deleting progress.

## Reward and Progress Guardrails

- Eligible acquisition-task awards are fixed at 4, 6, 8, 12, or 15 Seeds; no random multipliers.
- The Child sees any numeric award or “Parent recognition, no Seeds” before accepting.
- An accepted task earns its displayed award when completed with permitted help; only a smaller
  task agreed before acceptance may display a smaller award.
- Parent confirmation is idempotent; duplicate taps never create duplicate Seeds.
- A retry does not remove existing Seeds or show a failure badge.
- A missed day creates no debt and no broken-streak punishment.
- Trees never wither because of illness, travel, disability, access, or rest.
- Assistance is a completion mode, not a lesser moral score.
- `standard`/`fade-first` acquisition earns the displayed award and persistent eligible growth.
  Maintenance earns no Seeds or persistent landscape/canopy growth; eligible Green activity may
  still update a coarse circle action count. Only recurrent `fade-first` tasks prompt a Parent phase
  review after three confirmed completions. `recognition-only` uses phase `not_applicable` and never
  creates Seeds, persistent growth, canopy, or circle events. No transition is automatic, and all
  prior progress remains.
- Prayer, affection, family visits, food consumption, emotion regulation, and disability-related
  routines never enter a public or cross-family rank.
- No purchasable currency, loot boxes, ads, scarcity countdowns, or engagement-maximizing nudges.

## AI and Child-Safety Basis

UNICEF's 2025 guidance calls for age-appropriate child AI, clear disclosure, privacy and data agency,
protection from manipulation and dependency, human support, and safety/accountability.
[UNICEF Guidance on AI and Children 3.0](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/media/11991/file/UNICEF-Innocenti-Guidance-on-AI-and-Children-3-2025.pdf)

The APA advisory recommends protective defaults, reduced persuasive design, interaction limits,
human support, strong image/voice safeguards, and clear warnings that AI is not professional health
advice.
[APA AI and Adolescent Well-being Advisory](https://www.apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-ai-adolescent-well-being.pdf)

[OpenAI's Under 18 API Guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/safety-checks/under-18-api-guidance)
requires additional safeguards for minors, including age-appropriate disclosures and content,
monitoring/reporting and escalation mechanisms, and heightened care with data. It states that
personal data of children under 13 or the applicable digital-consent age should not be processed by
OpenAI services without zero data retention. A production implementation must re-check the current
official guidance rather than rely on this summary.

### Child Coach Invariants

- State that it is AI and can be wrong.
- Restrict the conversation to the current Parent-approved task.
- Ages 6–8 use curated intents without free text; ages 9–11 use structured intents/template input;
  ages 12–14 may use guardian-enabled bounded text or push-to-talk. No band gets unrestricted chat.
- Never invite secrets, exclusivity, attachment, dependence, or endless conversation.
- Never infer emotions from a face or voice, recognize a face, diagnose, or score personality.
- Use push-to-talk only; never ambient or background listening.
- Make photo/voice optional and tell the Child what the Parent can see before sending.
- For the MVP, use prepared synthetic media and deterministic responses as the always-available
  fallback. Only the Parent task-refinement path may make a real model call with synthetic input,
  through an approved secure server boundary, structured validation, and honest live/prepared
  labels. The Child Coach remains prepared in P0.
- Escalate ordinary task hazards to an adult. A future high-risk safeguarding path must support an
  alternate trusted adult and be reviewed before real deployment.

### Parent Guide Report Shape

1. **Window and facts:** observable completion/reminder data and time period.
2. **Strengths first:** specific actions or improvements.
3. **Pattern:** neutral change, explicitly uncertain and never labeled normal/abnormal.
4. **Question:** one open question the Parent can ask the Child.
5. **Adjustment:** smaller step, cue, choice, or environmental support.
6. **Outside help:** only when appropriate; suggest a pediatrician, school professional, or another
   qualified person without making a diagnosis.

Example: “During the last seven days, Salem completed two recycling tasks independently and asked
for help with the evening routine three times. Ask which step feels unclear. Consider showing only
one step after dinner this week.”

Prohibited outputs include lazy, defiant, abnormal, good/bad child, ADHD, anxiety, personality
scores, deception claims, emotion scores, parenting-quality judgments, and religious-worth scores.

## Privacy and Legal Review Triggers

The UAE's child digital-safety and personal-data frameworks are relevant to a real deployment.
The prototype must not claim compliance. Before any real child accounts, social sharing, or media
processing, obtain current UAE legal review and complete a child-rights/privacy impact assessment.

- [UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 26 of 2025 on Child Digital Safety](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/3912)
- [UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on Personal Data Protection](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972/download)

Minimum production questions include guardian consent and Child assent, age assurance, privacy by
default, minimum collection, separate media consent, task-scoped retention/deletion, no provider
training or secondary use, no biometric templates or face/voice identification, no emotion
inference, Parent visibility, trusted-adult escalation, family-circle access, reporting, withdrawal,
and provider data handling.

- Wedding phrases and customs across emirates, families, and event types.
- Majlis, coffee-service, dress, photography, and host-cue content.
